# Director's Idea Framework

Use this for conceptual explanation, teaching, and high-level diagnosis.

## Central Claim

Dancyger defines the director's idea as a deep subtextual interpretation that unifies production decisions. It is not the plot, not a decorative style, and not a general theme. A usable director's idea connects the main character, goal, world pressure, and emotional or philosophical stakes, then drives performance and camera choices.

## Levels of Directing

| Level | What it does | What it misses | Diagnostic question | Source notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Competent | Clarifies plot, genre, action, and audience orientation. | Subtext is thin; style may energize without deepening meaning. | Does the film communicate what happens but leave the deeper pressure underused? | `md/第3章_称职导演_总结.md` |
| Good | Finds a layered interpretation and makes actor/camera choices support that subtext. | May still rely on a limited or familiar authorial voice. | Do craft choices alter how the story means, not only how it moves? | `md/第4章_好导演_总结.md` |
| Great | Uses a bold, economical, deeply personal interpretation that transforms simple material into distinctive experience. | Risk of over-assertion or self-imitation if not tied to the text. | Does the director's voice reshape performance, image, rhythm, and audience experience? | `md/第5章_伟大导演_总结.md` |

## Director's Idea Test

1. State the story's visible conflict.
2. State the hidden pressure that makes the conflict matter.
3. Tie the hidden pressure to the main character's goal or wound.
4. Translate the pressure into actor behavior: arc, performance size, emotional access, relation to other characters.
5. Translate it into camera and design: proximity, objectivity/subjectivity, height, movement, lighting, sound, editing, and art direction.
6. Reject the idea if the performance and camera plan could serve many unrelated stories.

## Common Misuses

- Theme-only: "This is about love" is too broad unless it becomes a directing pressure such as romance as life force, doomed love, sexuality as power struggle, or love blocked by social form.
- Mood-only: darkness, nostalgia, energy, irony, or tenderness are results, not full director's ideas.
- Technique-first: handheld camera, close-ups, stylized lighting, or fast cutting must be justified by interpretation.
- Case copying: Eisenstein's dialectic, Ford's heroism, Kubrick's cool distance, and Spielberg's childlike wonder are not interchangeable recipes.

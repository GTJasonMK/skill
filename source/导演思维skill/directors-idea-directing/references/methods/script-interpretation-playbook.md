# Script Interpretation Playbook

Use this when the user asks how to apply the book to a script, scene, directing statement, actor note, or camera plan.

## Workflow

1. **Read for plot and character goal.** Identify protagonist, objective, obstacle, turning point, and ending pressure.
2. **Find interpretive axes.** Dancyger repeatedly uses interior/exterior, young/old, male/female, political/sociological/psychological, and tone as ways to expose subtext.
3. **Write candidate director's ideas.** Each candidate must connect hidden pressure to character action and audience experience.
4. **Choose performance strategy.** Define casting implications, character arc, outside-in or inside-out approach, emotional size, and relation between main and secondary characters.
5. **Choose camera strategy.** Decide shot scale, placement, height, movement, objectivity/subjectivity, lighting, art direction, sound, and editing emphasis.
6. **Run unity check.** Remove any craft choice that merely advertises style without supporting the idea.
7. **Name limits.** State what the script, genre, budget, actor capacity, or audience expectation may make unworkable.

## Interpretation Axes

| Axis | Useful question | Typical craft consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Interior / exterior | Is the core conflict internal, social, spatial, or physical? | Subjective camera, proximity, performance restraint, or environment emphasis. |
| Young / old | Is desire shaped by age, innocence, memory, decline, or generational pressure? | Casting contrast, rhythm, child/adult point of view, scale of performance. |
| Male / female | Is gender relation a source of power, romance, violence, or social expectation? | Blocking, gaze, camera distance, intimacy boundary. |
| Political / sociological / psychological | Is the story pressure institutional, group-based, or interior? | Public/private space, crowd vs individual, close-up vs tableau. |
| Tone | Is the same plot comic, tragic, romantic, horrific, satirical, or lyrical? | Performance pitch, lens/proximity, lighting, edit rhythm, sound world. |

## Actor Channel

- Use character arc as the road map for performance.
- Casting is interpretive: the actor's body, voice, persona, and vulnerability shape the director's idea.
- Secondary character arcs must serve or pressure the main character's arc.
- Outside-in choices emphasize physical behavior, mask, costume, gesture, and social role.
- Inside-out choices emphasize motivation, memory, emotion, and psychological access.

## Camera Channel

- Shot scale and proximity determine access to character, action, and environment.
- Objective placement can make the world feel social, historical, or morally observed.
- Subjective placement can bind the viewer to desire, fear, confusion, intoxication, or paranoia.
- Movement must have dramatic purpose: follow action, reveal relation, express pressure, or transition meaning.
- Lighting, art direction, sound, and editing are part of directing when they reinforce the idea.

## Validation Questions

- Can the idea be seen in both performance and camera choices?
- Does it change interpretation of the script rather than decorate it?
- Does the plan know what to omit, simplify, or underplay?
- Which Dancyger case is the closest precedent, and where does that precedent stop applying?
